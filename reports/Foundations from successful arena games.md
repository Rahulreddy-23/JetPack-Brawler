# Proven games back the core, expose six gaps

The evidence from shipped games mostly supports Jetpack Brawler's foundation. Successful arena and party games stay fun and reliable for the same reasons the GDD already uses: tight movement with small forgiveness windows, short rounds with almost no waiting, catch-up that everyone can see, a server that simulates from inputs only, and bots held to human limits. The GDD's movement ratios, its 0.1-second forgiveness windows and its dash distance are close to Celeste's published source code. Its server-authoritative model, its "host leaves, match ends" rule and its loser-picks card are also in line with developer practice. The research also finds six gaps. Two will break the prototype if nobody acts. First, the 4-card prototype pool cannot offer three cards at every pick in a 5-round match. Second, from Android 17, hotspot play will need a runtime permission for all local-network traffic, gameplay included. The other four need tuning values or rules from Rahul. The dash has no defined exit speed and pushes have no hitstun. "Last-placed" and "fewest points" can name different card pickers. The closing hazard has no warning cue. And "champions join the bot pool with the same behaviour" conflicts with "bots follow the same rules as players". The evidence is uneven. Celeste's movement constants and the Android and Godot rules come from source code and official documentation. Almost everything about Mini Militia, Brawl Stars and party-game balance comes from fan guides, reviews or search summaries. Many first numbers, including jetpack fuel, dash cooldown, aim-assist strength, stick dead zones, hazard timing and boss health, have no outside benchmark and must come from playtesting. The seventh note, the netfox v1.49.12 source study, had not arrived when this report was finished, so no claim here rests on netfox's internals.

**How to read the evidence labels.** Each claim carries one of these tags: **[source code]** (read from a game's published code), **[developer]** (a developer's own statement or talk), **[official docs]** (Android, Google Play or Godot documentation), **[community]** (forum posts, wikis, reviews, fan guides), **[search summary]** (seen only in a search-result extract; the page was not read), or **[inference]** (the researchers' or this report's reasoning, not stated by any source). Where `docs/DECISIONS.md` has settled a point, this report judges the evidence against that decision, not against the older GDD text. Every "proposal" below is a suggestion for Rahul. None of them has been made.

## Movement already matches Celeste's ratios; momentum and push recovery are undefined

The best-documented reference for tight 2D movement is Celeste's player code. Its run speed, jump speed and fall cap are 90, 105 and 160 px/s, a ratio of **1 : 1.17 : 1.78** [source code] ([Celeste Player.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)). The GDD's run 6, jetpack climb 7 and fall cap 12 (in bot heights per second) give **1 : 1.17 : 2.0**. The shape is almost the same; the GDD is about 27% slower in absolute run speed, which suits a shooter where players aim while moving [inference]. Celeste's coyote window is `JumpGraceTime = 0.1` seconds [source code], matching the GDD's 0.1-second forgiveness windows. Maddy Thorson lists ten such invisible helpers, including jump buffering, a half-gravity jump peak and corner correction, and says the team did not invent any of them [developer] ([Celeste & Forgiveness](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html)). The GDD dash of 3 bot heights in 0.15 seconds is nearly Celeste's (240 px/s for 0.15 s, about 3.3 heights) [source code]. Bullet immunity for the whole dash matches Hollow Knight's Shade Cloak, which still lets hazards hurt [community] ([Hollow Knight Wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Shade_Cloak)). The GDD rule that dash immunity covers bullets only is therefore consistent with that precedent.

The gaps are in values the GDD never sets, and these decide "tight" versus "floaty" more than top speeds do. Celeste reaches full run speed in about **0.09 seconds** (`RunAccel = 1000` against `MaxRun = 90`). It stops in about 0.225 seconds, and air acceleration is **0.65 times** ground acceleration [source code]. The GDD gives no gravity, ground acceleration, braking or air acceleration. It promises both "full left and right steering in the air" and "momentum carries". If steering works at full ground strength, a player can cancel dash momentum and knockback almost at once [inference]. The dash's "then normal momentum resumes" is also undefined. Celeste sets the exit speed to two-thirds of dash speed (`EndDashSpeed = 160` against `DashSpeed = 240`) [source code]. Without something like that, a 20-heights-per-second dash snaps back to the run cap of 6, and the GDD's "dash into a jetpack burn flings the bot further" will not happen [inference]. Two GDD timings also fall between ticks at 30 Hz: 0.15 seconds is 4.5 ticks, both for the jetpack ramp and for the dash. The notes suggest 4 or 5 ticks [inference]. The wall-jump has no values at all. Celeste's source gives a complete set: 130 px/s sideways, a 0.16-second forced-direction lockout so the stick cannot steer straight back into the wall, and a 2 to 3 px contact window [source code]. Scaled to the GDD's speeds, that is about 8.6 heights per second sideways and a 5-tick lockout [inference].

Knockback is the GDD's core, and here the evidence gives both support and a warning. Smash's datamined formula raises knockback in a straight line with damage taken and has no cap. In a worked example, a weak move launches about 5.5 times further at 150% than at 10% [community] ([SmashWiki, Knockback](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Knockback)). The GDD's maximum of 2 times is mild by comparison, which is reasonable because health also ends fights here [inference]. The lead's call reads "up to double below 25 health" as a straight-line ramp from 1 at 25 health to 2 at 0. That avoids the jump a step function would cause, and it handles the +20 knockout repair without a sudden change. Smash adds two things the GDD lacks. Hitstun lasts **0.4 frames per knockback unit**, so a pushed fighter cannot act at once. Directional influence lets a pushed fighter bend the launch by about **18 degrees in Melee and 9.74 degrees in Smash 4 and Ultimate** [community] ([SmashWiki, Hitstun](https://ssbwiki.com/Hitstun); [Directional influence](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Directional_influence)). Without some recovery window, a bot hit by melee can fire its jetpack straight away and cancel the push. That weakens "everything pushes" [inference]. Hit-freeze is also short. Melee's hitlag formula, floor(damage/3 + 3), gives about 6 frames for a 10-damage hit, and the cap is 20 frames in Melee and 30 from Brawl on. The GDD's 2 to 3 frames is below Melee's minimum for any damaging hit [community] ([SmashWiki, Hitlag](https://ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). The lead's call to freeze only the victim's picture, never the simulation, matches the notes' own reasoning that freezing the simulation for two bots is hard to predict over the network [inference].

Juice has only qualitative support. Jan Willem Nijman's "Art of Screenshake" talk improved a dull shooter with about 30 small changes, such as shaking the screen opposite to the direction of fire and making enemies explode at random. No frame counts or shake strengths were given [developer, reported] ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). The GDD already covers hit pause, shake with a reduce setting, flash and slow motion. Directional shake, shooter recoil and muzzle flash are presentation changes, not new features [inference].

**Proposals for Rahul (movement):** (1) Add ground acceleration, braking, air acceleration and gravity to the config resource, starting near Celeste's proportions: about 0.1 seconds to full run, about 0.2 seconds to stop, air at about 0.65 times ground, and gravity of 60 to 80 heights per second squared [inference from source code]. (2) Set a dash exit speed of about two-thirds of dash speed [source code]. (3) Decide whether a strong push (melee, scrap blast, blast centre) briefly reduces air steering and jetpack thrust, scaled to push strength, with some steering left. This adds a rule, so it is Rahul's call. If it is outside phase 1, log it in `docs/IDEAS.md` [inference from Smash]. (4) Scale hit-freeze with push strength, from about 2 frames up to 6 to 8 frames at melee and blast centres, with no freeze on bolt-thrower and nail-gun hits [inference from Smash]. (5) Say what "a bot that has just left a ledge can still wall-jump for 0.1 seconds" means: grace after leaving a ledge, or after leaving a wall [inference]. (6) Test the 3-second dash cooldown against 1.5 seconds. No reference supports 3 seconds: Celeste uses 0.2 seconds plus a ground refill, and Hollow Knight's Shade Cloak 1.5 seconds [source code; community]. (7) Corner correction (Celeste: 4 px, about 0.36 character heights) is missing from the GDD. For a jetpack game that climbs into ceiling lips, it is probably the next forgiveness helper that matters. Log it in `docs/IDEAS.md` [inference].

## Netcode practice confirms the host model; Android 17 changes the hotspot plan

The pattern in shipped shooters matches the GDD's Tech approach closely. Each input carries a sequence number. The client predicts its own movement, resets to the server state when it arrives and replays any inputs the server has not yet seen. Irreversible events such as death wait for server confirmation [developer] ([Gambetta, Client-Side Prediction](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.html)). That matches the lead's call that each phone predicts only its own bot's movement and the look of its own shots. Overwatch sends every unacknowledged input in each packet, so a lost packet's inputs arrive in the next one, and the server repeats the last input when none arrives [search summary of the GDC 2017 talk] ([Edgegap, Overwatch](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-overwatch-2016-netcode-architecture-rollback)). Both techniques fit a 30 Hz input stream on lossy hotspot Wi-Fi [inference].

For showing other players "in the past", Valve's default of **100 ms** equals two snapshot intervals at Source's default update rate, so one lost snapshot never stalls the picture [official docs, search extract] ([Valve wiki, Interpolation](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Interpolation)). Glenn Fiedler's loss-tolerant rule of thumb gives **150 ms at 30 packets per second** [developer] ([Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)). At the GDD's 30 ticks, a starting interpolation delay of about 100 ms (3 ticks) sits between the two. At the 150 ms ping ceiling, the host would then need to rewind about 175 ms when judging hits [inference]. Shipped games cap the rewind. Overwatch turns off hit prediction above about 220 ms round trip so targets behind cover are not killed by long rewinds [search summary]. Gambetta calls favouring the shooter "somewhat unfair" to the victim but better than missing shots that clearly landed [developer] ([Gambetta, Lag Compensation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/lag-compensation.html)). No source fetched covers slow projectiles or networked knockback directly. The notes infer that the host should spawn projectiles, with a cosmetic copy on the shooter's phone, and should send each knockback impulse tagged with its tick so the victim's phone can replay from that tick [inference]. This is the area where the missing netfox study would have helped most.

For player hosts, the evidence supports the GDD's "host leaves, match ends; tap Host Rematch". Halo Reach's host migration is described as "a significant engineering undertaking" [search summary] ([Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)). The gap is the backgrounded host. Android's Doze limits network access whenever the screen turns off, from Android 7 on [official docs] ([Android Doze](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)), and mobile operating systems close the sockets of apps left in the background [community] ([Heroic Labs forum](https://forum.heroiclabs.com/t/not-able-to-find-match-after-keeping-app-in-background/5074)). A host phone that takes a call is a lost host. Godot 4.7 provides `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_PAUSED` and `NOTIFICATION_WM_GO_BACK_REQUEST` to detect this and the Back button [official docs] ([Godot Node reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_node.html)). Whether ENet keeps running while paused is unknown and must be tested on a device.

The single most important finding in the netcode note is new platform policy. From **Android 17 (API 37)**, apps that target SDK 37 or higher are blocked from **all** local-network traffic until the user grants the runtime permission `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK`. The block covers TCP and UDP, unicast, broadcast and multicast, in both directions. UDP sends then fail with EPERM [official docs] ([Android, Local network permission](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/local-network-permission)). This affects ENet gameplay on a hotspot, not only discovery. Apps that target below 37 get the permission automatically and must not request it. Google Play currently requires target API 36 for new apps and updates, from 31 August 2026 [official docs] ([Play target API](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). The exact date when Play will require API 37 was not checked. The GDD's Hotspot row says only "found by local network broadcast". Discovery itself has documented traps. Since Android 11, the hotspot subnet is not fixed, so the old 192.168.43.1 address cannot be hard-coded [community] ([SoftApHelper](https://github.com/zimq/SoftApHelper)). Broadcasting to 255.255.255.255 is reported to fail when the phone is itself the hotspot; a broadcast to its own subnet works [community] ([Chainfire](https://chainfire.eu/articles/742/_dev_Android_Datagrams_UDP_Broadcast_and_Multicast_...)). Some devices receive broadcasts only while the app holds a Wi-Fi `MulticastLock`, which drains the battery while held [official docs] ([MulticastLock](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/WifiManager.MulticastLock)). Godot's docs say some Android devices need `CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE` for broadcast [official docs] ([Godot PacketPeerUDP](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_packetpeerudp.html)). One Godot user's phone host failed because it showed an IPv6 address [community] ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/the-mobile-becoming-server-in-local-multiplayer-games-is-a-chaos/53979)). For phase 1's join-by-IP, the practical rules are: bind ENet to IPv4, read the host's real interface address when hosting, and show that address [inference]. This evidence turns the GDD's QR or code fallback from a nice-to-have into a requirement [inference].

The cheating evidence is indirect. No technical analysis of Mini Militia's protocol was found. Mod listings advertise unlimited health, ammo and jetpack fuel, which suggests the client held those values [community] ([itch.io mod listing](https://rakibmahmud6514.itch.io/mini-militia-mod-by-sk-shohidullah)). Those are the values the GDD keeps on the host. The notes derive a minimum check list: clamp input axes to −1 to 1; accept one input per tick per player; enforce fire rate on the host's clock; keep ammo and fuel on the host; check pickups at the host's tick; and clamp a client's claimed shot time to the rewind cap [inference]. The bandwidth estimate is about 5 to 6 KB/s down per client and 40 to 50 KB/s up from the host with eight players, well within hotspot Wi-Fi. That is arithmetic, not a measurement [inference].

**Proposals for Rahul (netcode):** (1) Add to the hotspot design that targeting Android 17 needs `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` for gameplay traffic, with an explanation screen before the request and a clear error message if it is denied. Until the target SDK is raised to 37, do not request it [official docs]. (2) Treat a backgrounded or screen-off host as a lost host after a set timeout: show "Host paused", then end the match cleanly [official docs; community]. (3) Put the interpolation delay and a rewind cap in the config resource [developer; official docs]. (4) Decide per weapon type whether to favour the shooter or the victim. A rewind for slow, visible rockets and bombs is a design choice no source settles [inference]. (5) Run the same host validation in hotspot mode as online, because the code path is shared [inference].

## Touch controls follow genre precedent, except the overloaded right stick

The control scheme has precedent for its main parts. Up-for-jetpack on the left stick comes from Mini Militia, whose jetpack reviewers treat as its signature, not as a control problem [search summary] ([Wikipedia, Mini Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Militia)). Fixed sticks are argued to be clearer for first-timers. A developer who chose static sticks says a floating stick "may not be easily understood" without a visual cue [developer] ([Game Developer, on-screen thumb sticks](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/from-keyboard-and-mouse-to-on-screen-analog-thumb-sticks)). Call of Duty Mobile exposes Fixed Joystick and a drag-and-resize layout editor with opacity control [developer] ([Activision](https://blog.activision.com/call-of-duty/2019-10/Getting-a-Grip-on-the-Call-of-Duty-Mobile-Controls)). Brawl Stars added customisable controls in April 2020, and players said it stopped mis-taps [community] ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2020/04/14/brawl-stars-customizable-controls-update-now-live)). On aim assist, the strongest developer evidence is from War Robots. Its optional, transparent auto-aim was used by only **18%** of players at first, but **80%** of those who tried it kept it, and aiming complaints fell noticeably [developer] ([Game Developer, War Robots auto-aim](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-create-a-fair-auto-aiming-system-in-a-robot-shooter-)). That supports the GDD's light snap that can be switched off, provided the rule is simple and predictable [inference].

The weak spot is the right stick. It carries aim, fire past the ring, release-to-fire for charged weapons and quick-tap melee. In Brawl Stars, a tap on the attack stick fires an auto-aimed shot [community, search summary] ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/Brawl-Stars/archives/316910)), so players arriving from it may expect a tap to shoot. No precedent for tap-for-melee was found. A 2013 mobile-platformer postmortem argues that virtual sticks "require too much directional nuance" without physical feedback [developer, opinion] ([Game Developer, Mikey Shorts](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/mikey-shorts-a-case-study-of-optimizing-controls-for-mobile-platformers)). The left stick's extra duties, down to drop through platforms and away from a wall to wall-jump, also have no precedent in the sources. A thumb running sideways with a slight upward drift can fire the jetpack by accident [inference].

Platform guidance fills in sizes and placement that the GDD leaves open. Thumb-tap research recommends about **9.2 to 9.6 mm** targets [developer, research] ([Parhi et al., Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150)). Nielsen Norman Group puts a thumb's contact area at about 2.5 cm and recommends about 2 cm for primary actions in fast-moving use [official guidance] ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)). Android's 48 dp minimum is only about 7.6 mm, so action buttons of about 56 to 64 dp and sticks near 130 dp make sense as starting values [inference]. Android says not to place touch targets under gesture-navigation insets. Games may exclude edge areas with `setSystemGestureExclusionRects()` during play, but the bottom home gesture cannot be blocked [official docs] ([Android gesture navigation](https://developer.android.com/training/gestures/gesturenav); [System bars](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/foundations/system-bars)). Android's haptics guidance is "less is more": if the only choice is buzzy haptics or none, choose none [official docs] ([Android haptics](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/haptics/haptics-principles)). Godot 4.7 ships a new `VirtualJoystick` node with Fixed, Dynamic and Following modes [official docs] ([Godot 4.7 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)). It should be evaluated before custom sticks are written.

For onboarding, the only measured data is old and generic. In devtodev's 2017 cases, a skippable tutorial passed about **75%** of players and a forced one about **40%**, with far more players quitting during the forced one [developer, analytics] ([Game Developer / devtodev](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/how-to-optimize-the-tutorial-for-your-game)). Brawl Stars reportedly uses a short required tutorial followed by a match against bots [community] ([LDShop](https://www.ldshop.gg/blog/brawl-stars-gp/brawl-stars-beginner's-guide-2025.html)). That flow suits the GDD's Easy bots [inference].

**Proposals for Rahul (controls):** (1) Show a separate melee button by default for first sessions. The GDD currently offers it only as an option for experienced players, so this changes the GDD's default [inference]. (2) Count "up" only within an angle cone around vertical, and give "down" a larger dead zone than "up". Keep both in config and test them with timing logs [inference]. (3) Give charged weapons a distinct charge ring, so it is visible that one stick has two ways to fire [inference]. (4) Make aim assist use the same line-of-sight test as bots, so it never locks on through walls [inference from War Robots]. (5) Base button sizes on physical millimetres and keep controls inset from the safe area [official docs; research]. (6) Use short, skippable bot practice with hints that appear in play, not a long forced tutorial [developer, analytics].

## Party rounds work as designed, but the card pool and the picker rule break

The structure sits inside the range developers describe as working. Ultimate Chicken Horse's designers kept run phases under 20 seconds so "if players fail early, they don't have to wait very long" [developer] ([Game Developer, UCH](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-clever-endeavour-games-i-ultimate-chicken-horse-i-)). A ROUNDS game lasts about 10 minutes [community] ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/i-hate-1v1-competitive-games-but-rounds-is-the-unhinged-exception)). ROUNDS is the direct precedent for loser-picks. Reviewers call it non-punishing and self-balancing [community] ([Rounds Network](https://rounds.network/rounds-review-my-new-favorite-multiplayer-roguelike/)). It holds 96% positive of about 25,900 Steam reviews [search summary]. The only pacing complaint found was waiting while the opponent picks [community] ([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/rounds/user-reviews/)). The 5-second timer with a random fallback addresses that. DECISIONS.md replaces the GDD's "under 5 seconds" gap with **about 8 seconds** (2-second replay, 5-second pick, 1-second start), so a match runs roughly 4.5 to 5 minutes. Ludo King's 5-minute quick mode is weak but relevant support for that length in India [community] ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/ludo-king-takes-over-the-indian-mobile-games-market/)). Matt Thorson warns that "if the game is tuned well, it doesn't actually require that much variation to be satisfying" [developer] ([Game Developer, TowerFall](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-magic-of-i-towerfall-i-depth-simplicity-community)). That supports the phase-1 rule that the core 45-second fight must be fun with no card at all.

The card differs from ROUNDS in a way that matters. ROUNDS cards are personal and stack, which gives depth and also produces the combo complaints (homing plus ricochet) [community] ([Bits and Pieces](https://bitsandpieces.games/2024/02/23/rounds/)). Chaos Cards apply to everyone and replace each other. That removes snowballing, but it also leaves the picker little personal help [inference]. Ultimate Chicken Horse found that players who fell behind "couldn't catch up". Its fix was an explicit, visible "Underdog" comeback bonus, given to a player who has not reached the goal for two or more turns while others have [developer] ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/ultimate-chicken-horse-from-game-jam-prototype-to-hilarious-party-game); [Clever Endeavour rules](https://cleverendeavourgames.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/32000028991-custom-rules-and-presets)). Catch-up is accepted when it is visible and not aimed at the leader. Mario Kart's blue shell, built so "everyone was in it until the end", stays controversial because it targets the leader [developer, via Wikipedia] ([Blue shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_shell)). A global card cannot target anyone, which avoids that complaint. The open risk runs the other way: the picker may not feel any comeback at all [inference].

The document's own numbers produce two defects. **First, the pool arithmetic.** A 5-round match has four picks, after rounds 1 to 4. With the prototype's four cards (Moon Boots, Glass Cannon, Free Fuel, Ice Rink), three offered and each picked card leaving the pool: pick 2 offers the only three cards left, pick 3 has two, and pick 4 has one. Offering three cards at every pick needs **at least 6 cards** for 5 rounds and **8 cards** for 7-round custom rooms [inference, arithmetic]. Classic Brawl's card every minute raises the same question. **Second, the picker.** "The Twist" says "last-placed player", while Match flow says "player with the fewest points". The first could mean last in this round; the second means lowest total. The same low scorer could pick every round [inference]. Before round 1 everyone has zero points, so the "knocked out earliest" tiebreak decides [inference].

Scoring leans heavily on kills by design. In Apex's ALGS, first place equals 12 kills; in one Fortnite FNCS Community Cup it equalled 25 [search summary] ([SI, ALGS](https://www.si.com/esports/apex-legends/apex-legends-global-series-match-point-explained); [Epic, FNCS rules](https://www.fortnite.com/competitive/news/fncs-community-cup-chapter-4-season-3-official-rules)). In Jetpack Brawler it equals 1.5 kills. With eight players, the seven knockouts in a round are worth 14 points against 6 points for all placements. In the **4-player prototype**, three knockouts are worth 6 points, the same as the placement total, so the balance shifts with lobby size [inference, arithmetic]. The GDD's anti-hiding goal holds, but winning a round counts for little [inference]. For the hazard, Bomberman warns "Hurry Up!" before walls drop [community, search summary] ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Super_Bomberman/Gameplay)). Bomberman R declares a draw when the hazard crushes the last players together [developer, manual] ([Konami manual](https://www.konami.com/games/bomberman/r/us/en/manual/league.html)). The GDD states neither a warning cue for the rising metal nor what happens when it kills the last two bots on the same tick. The lead's trade rule covers bots knocking each other out, not the hazard.

On eliminated players, Innersloth admits "If you get killed first that sucks. Your game is boring" [developer] ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/among-us-ghost-update)). Bomberman's revenge carts let dead players keep throwing bombs from the edge [developer, manual] ([Konami](https://www.konami.com/games/bomberman/r/us/en/manual/league.html)). UCH awards "postmortem" points [developer]. The tension, as reported, is that powers which affect the game can spoil it, while powers which don't are "just a mini-game" [community] ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/among-us-developer-s-discuss-changes-ghosts-can)). The Scrap Ghost sits in the middle. Whether ghost bolts count toward match points is unspecified, and that choice decides how much ghosts can influence the result [inference]. Ghosts are not in DECISIONS' phase 1 or phase 2 list, so this matters for first release.

**Proposals for Rahul (rounds):** (1) Either raise the prototype pool to at least six cards, allow repeats once the pool runs low, or offer fewer than three when the pool is small. Each option is a scope or rule change [inference, arithmetic]. (2) Pick one meaning for "last place": lowest total points, or lowest in this round [inference]. (3) Add a visible warning (screen and sound) before the hazard rises, and a rule for the last two bots dying to the hazard together [developer, manual]. (4) Decide whether a light, visible catch-up should exist beyond the card, in the style of UCH's Underdog, after the phase-2 playtest shows whether trailing players give up [developer]. (5) Add a cheap tiebreak before sudden death, such as round wins and then total knockouts, as Fortnite layers its tiebreaks [search summary]. (6) Decide whether ghost bolts are coins only or also match points [inference]. (7) The lead's call counts hazard time from the end of the countdown. Since DECISIONS removes the countdown after round 1, confirm that later rounds count from the end of the 1-second start [inference].

## Human-limited bots are proven; champions in the bot pool are not

The two levers the project allows, reaction time and aim error, are the levers the research names. Laird and Duchi's Soar Quakebot study is a small pilot with 8 judges. A bot with a **0.10-second** decision time was rated most human (6.3 of 10) and still reached a skill ratio of **0.625** against an expert. Bots at 0.25 and 0.5 seconds looked worse because they crashed into walls. Judges did not penalise aiming at the enemy's current position, but did penalise perfect target leading [developer, research] ([Laird and Duchi 2001](https://www.qrg.northwestern.edu/Resources/aigames.org/2001/Laird.pdf)). Quake III stores reaction time, aim accuracy and turn speed as per-skill settings, interpolating between levels [official docs] ([Q3 bot manual](https://icculus.org/gtkradiant/documentation/bot_manual/characteristics.html)). Its author disliked an earlier bot whose top level "just couldn't lose", while its lower levels only aimed worse and "did less intelligent things" [developer, interview] ([TU Delft Outlook](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_f7fa0a9a-1cb3-441b-bd12-f1d6426be5b1)). Measured simple visual reaction time averages about **190 ms** for college-age adults [community, citing a lab review] ([Mental chronometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_chronometry)). Soren Johnson finds that players call hidden advantages "cheating", while visible balancing feels acceptable [developer] ([Game Developer, Cheatin' Hearts](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-game-ai-our-cheatin-hearts)).

This supports the GDD's bot rules and settles an apparent tension: Very Hard bots can "beat most players" at human speed by being consistent (no wasted fuel, good positioning), not by being faster [inference from Laird]. Hard bots' "lead moving targets" should use a delayed, noisy speed estimate [inference]. Easy bots need poor decisions as well as poor aim, which the "which skills the bot uses" lever allows [inference from van Waveren]. Starting numbers in the notes are proposals, not sourced values. Reaction time would be about 200 to 250 ms for Very Hard, 300 to 400 ms for Hard and Medium, and 450 to 700 ms for Easy, varied per event. Aim noise would be roughly 8 to 12 degrees for Easy down to 1 to 1.5 degrees for Very Hard [inference]. For architecture, shipped bots are cheap and layered. Left 4 Dead separates a Vision component, which owns line of sight and field of view, from Intention, a state machine [developer] ([Booth, L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). The Half-Life FlexBot SDK runs bots as "fake clients" that produce the same action vector as a player [developer, research] ([Khoo et al. 2002](https://www.qrg.northwestern.edu/Resources/aigames.org/papers2002/SSS202AKhoo.pdf)). Soldat's bots follow hand-placed waypoints with instructions such as "Use Jetpack" [community] ([Soldat wiki](https://wiki.soldat.pl/index.php/WayPoint)). For this game the notes suggest: each bot writes the same input structure as a client into the host's input queue; brains update at about 10 Hz, staggered across ticks; and navigation uses a precomputed coarse grid with distance fields instead of runtime pathfinding [inference]. Left 4 Dead also ran bots at accelerated time for automated stress tests [developer], which maps directly onto the project's headless-run check.

For bosses, the evidence is principles without numbers. Cuphead's team built a phase's attack pattern before its art. They simplified an animation that was "too visually distracting" so the attack read clearly [developer] ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2022/12/27/how-studio-mdhr-builds-a-cuphead-boss)). The reporter adds that the team avoids making final phases the longest or hardest. Trade-press opinion says 2 to 3 phases suit most bosses and that a missing telegraph feels cheap [community] ([Game Developer, Boss Battle Design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/boss-battle-design-and-structure)). The only splitting-boss example found, Mega Man's 19-piece Yellow Devil, draws complaints about memorisation and a tiny weak point, not about the split itself [community] ([Mega Man Maker wiki](https://wiki.megamanmaker.com/index.php/Yellow_Devil)). Dabba's three tins are within a manageable range. The risk is tedium from three identical tins each needing many hits [inference]. Proposals in the notes include: give each tin different or staggered behaviour; split the stack's health so the three tins together hold about 75 to 90% of it; let survivors speed up when a tin dies; and keep the patterns mostly fixed so first-timers can learn them [inference].

The biggest design conflict in this area comes from the GDD itself. "Beaten champions join the bot pool in multiplayer with the same behaviour", yet bots "follow the same rules as players: the same health", and every bot has the same hit area and health. Dabba's split gives one bot three hit areas. Fanatic's blade shield, Static's invisibility and Mixie's vortex are all abilities players do not have [inference from GDD text]. No shipped precedent for rule-bound boss bots was found. Dabba is in phase 1 and bots fill seats in phase 2, so this needs an answer before phase 2.

**Proposals for Rahul (bots and bosses):** (1) Decide what a champion is in multiplayer: a normal bot wearing the champion's look, or its trick turned into equipment any player could pick up. Alternatively, keep champions out of the bot pool until Boss Raid, which the GDD already lists as Later [inference]. (2) Give first-time players easier games through the mix of bot levels in the lobby, not hidden stat changes. PUBG Mobile reportedly does this, though the publisher has not confirmed it [community] ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/pubg-mobile-bots/)). (3) Set Dabba's par time near the 60th to 70th percentile of playtest completion times, and tune so a median first-timer wins using one or two rebuilds [inference].

## Godot and Android practice supports DECISIONS.md and adds three deadlines

The renderer decision is backed by official documentation. Godot's renderer overview recommends Compatibility when "you want the best performance possible on all devices", lists 2D games as a reason to choose it, and marks Mobile as slower on old and low-end hardware [official docs] ([Godot renderers](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html)). Forum reports of Vulkan crashes and black screens on Mali and Adreno phones add community support [community] ([GitHub issue 67430](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/67430)). The quality note recorded `project.godot` as using the Mobile renderer. A read-only check while writing this report found `rendering_method="gl_compatibility"`, so the project now matches the lead's call. Compatibility lacks particle trails, 2D HDR and compute shaders, none of which the GDD needs [official docs; inference]. No published frame-time or temperature comparison on budget phones exists, so a 20-minute soak on Rahul's cheapest phone is the real test [inference].

For testing, GUT **9.7.1** (on its `godot_4_7` branch) and gdUnit4 **6.2.x** both support Godot 4.7, and gdUnit4's scene runner can simulate touch [official docs, project READMEs] ([GUT](https://github.com/bitwes/Gut); [gdUnit4](https://github.com/godot-gdunit-labs/gdUnit4)). Godot's `--headless`, `--import`, `--fixed-fps` and `--export-debug` flags support a minimal CI run. `--fixed-fps 30` matches the 30-tick simulation and makes runs repeatable [official docs] ([Godot command line](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html)). Any GDScript warning can be made an error; the project already does this for `untyped_declaration` [official docs] ([warning system](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/gdscript/warning_system.html)). gdtoolkit's latest release is 4.5.0 from October 2025, so it may fail to parse 4.7-only syntax [official docs] ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/gdtoolkit/)). The engine's own `--check-only` is the safer static gate [inference].

Three external deadlines apply. **Target API 36** has been required for new apps and updates since 31 August 2026 [official docs] ([Play target API](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). Godot 4.7's documented toolchain lists platform android-35, so whether the export can target 36 needs checking [official docs] ([Godot Android export](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_android.html)). From **1 February 2027**, Play will not accept updates that lack 16 KB page-size support. Godot 4.7's NDK r28b aligns builds by default, but the AAB should still be checked with `zipalign` or `bundletool` [official docs] ([16 KB pages](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)). Personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days** before production access [official docs] ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). That is a real calendar item for Rahul, because publishing is his decision. After launch, Android vitals flag a user-perceived crash rate above **1.09%** or an ANR rate above **0.47%** [official docs] ([Android vitals](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/vitals)).

For download size, Godot's size guide lists stripping debug symbols (5 to 10 times smaller binary), `lto=full`, `optimize=size_extra`, the fallback text server and `disable_3d` (about 15%) [official docs] ([Optimizing for size](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/development/compiling/optimizing_for_size.html)). An arm64 AAB should stay well under 80 MB, but no measured stock 4.7 template size exists; reported sizes are community anecdotes [community]. Crash capture with no backend can rely on `adb logcat`, Godot file logging and Play vitals. The Sentry Godot SDK captures native and script errors [search summary] ([Sentry Godot](https://docs.sentry.io/platforms/godot/)), but it is a paid third-party service and so Rahul's decision. Performance advice is official only at the level of principle: profile on more than one device, and frame time is set by the slower of CPU and GPU [official docs] ([general optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/general_optimization.html)). The 12 ms CPU and 12 ms GPU budget, the `Engine.max_fps = 60` cap for 90 and 120 Hz screens, and pooling bullets and particles are engineering practice, not sourced figures [inference]. One rule ties directly to the card design. Resources loaded from disk are shared between instances, so the Chaos Card match-settings resource must be one server-owned instance that per-player state never writes to [inference].

**Proposals for Rahul (quality):** (1) Choose a device test set with one phone per GPU family (PowerVR, Mali, Adreno), if he can borrow them [inference]. (2) Decide whether to keep 32-bit armeabi-v7a builds [inference]. (3) Decide on Sentry or another paid crash service [search summary]. (4) Plan the 14-day, 12-tester closed test into the first-release schedule [official docs].

## What could not be found, and what playtesting must supply

The missing pieces are specific, and several are core tuning numbers. **No source gave Mini Militia's or Soldat's jetpack fuel, thrust or refill values.** The GDD's 2.5 seconds of thrust and 2-second refill have no outside comparison, and a frame-count measurement from gameplay video would still be only a fan measurement. No dash cooldown reference near 3 seconds was found. Celeste's input buffer length lives in a file that could not be retrieved, and no TowerFall movement values are public. **No stick dead zone, stick diameter, outer-ring radius or aim-assist strength was found for any mobile shooter.** There is no error-rate data for overloaded sticks, no usage rate for layout editors, and no data on multi-touch limits or palm rejection on budget Indian phones. On rounds, there are no numbers for crate spawn timing, hazard start relative to round length, ROUNDS card balance or Mini Militia retention. Steam reviews, Reddit and most developer postmortems for ROUNDS, Stick Fight, Duck Game and Move or Die could not be retrieved, so how common any complaint is remains unknown. On bots, there is no aim-error data by skill tier, no GDScript cost benchmark for seven bots on a phone that is also hosting, no shipped precedent for boss-as-bot, and no telegraph durations or phase health splits from Cuphead, Hollow Knight or Furi. On the platform side, the following were not checked: the date Play will require API 37; how the hotspot subnet is randomised on Samsung, Xiaomi and Realme phones; whether Godot 4.7 keeps ENet running while paused; whether its Android template acquires a `MulticastLock`; and a measured stock template size. None of the lead's calls on trades, the 3-second knockout credit or render-only hit-freeze could be checked against an outside source. Finally, **the netfox v1.49.12 source study (`netfox_patterns.md`) was still absent when this report was finished.** So nothing here confirms how netfox handles input redundancy, knockback reconciliation, rewind caps or bot input injection. Those points are marked as inference above and should be checked against netfox when that note lands.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's risk picture more than its design. The GDD's feel numbers are already close to the best-documented reference. The real risks are in places the GDD treats as settled or does not mention: a card pool that runs out before the match ends, a hotspot path that Android 17 will close by default, and a champion bot pool that breaks the game's own fairness rule. Each can be fixed cheaply now and would be costly to find at the phase-2 gate. The other lesson is about where proof will come from. For the numbers that decide whether this game is fun (fuel, dash cooldown, push recovery, dead zones, assist strength, hazard timing), no outside source exists. Phase 1 should therefore log every tuning value it tests, keep them all in the config resource, and treat Rahul's two-phone sessions as the primary evidence, not as confirmation of a benchmark.
